# The Science

The apps let you work with real seismic data. These pages explain the science behind them:
what you are looking at on a seismogram, and why the method finds the quake. They are short
and build on each other, so read them in order the first time.

1. **[Seismic waves](waves.md):** what P and S waves are and how they move the rock.<br>
   *Used in: [Wave Race](../apps/wave-race.md), [Quake Waves](locating.md#watch-it-happen).*
2. **[Reading a seismogram](seismograms.md):** how a seismometer records shaking, the parts of
   a quake record, and what else shows up (ocean hum, wind, people, blasts, distant quakes,
   instrument problems), with filters and spectrograms to pull them apart.<br>
   *Used in: [Wave Race](../apps/wave-race.md); School Pulse (coming soon).*
3. **[Distance from S minus P](s-minus-p.md):** how the time between P and S tells you how far
   away the quake was, and why the depth matters.<br>
   *Used in: [Wave Race](../apps/wave-race.md), [Quake Waves](locating.md#watch-it-happen).*
4. **[Locating a quake](locating.md):** how distances from several stations pin down the
   epicenter, and how professionals do it.<br>
   *Used in: [Wave Race](../apps/wave-race.md), [Quake Waves](locating.md#watch-it-happen).*
5. **[Our stations and data](stations.md):** which seismometers the apps use, who runs them,
   and how to credit the data in your own work.<br>
   *Used in: [Wave Race](../apps/wave-race.md).*
