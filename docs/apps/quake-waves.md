# Quake Waves

Watch P and S waves race out from a quake to four stations, and see how the gap between them
finds the quake.

[Open Quake Waves](../app/quake-waves/index.html){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

!!! abstract "At a glance"
    - **You will:** watch P and S waves reach four stations, and their S − P times turn into
      circles that cross at the quake.
    - **Time:** about 10 seconds per run at the starting speed; 5–10 minutes with the
      questions below.
    - **Works on:** any browser, including Chromebooks. No install, no accounts.
    - **The science:** [Seismic waves](../theory/waves.md) · [Distance from S minus P](../theory/s-minus-p.md) · [Locating a quake](../theory/locating.md)
    - **For teachers:** [One quake, two waves](../teachers/one-quake-two-waves.md) (a 10-minute demo) · [Quake Locator activity card](../teachers/wave-race-card.md) (as the warm-up) · [Standards](../teachers/standards.md#quake-waves)
    - **Data:** none. A cartoon: the seismograms are made up, but the arrival times are exact
      (P 5.8 km/s, S 3.3 km/s, as in Quake Locator).

## What you see

- **The map:** the quake (orange star), stations A to D (triangles that shake as the waves
  pass), the **P wave** (blue ring) and the **S wave** (red ring).
- **The seismograms:** one row per station, drawn as the waves arrive. When S arrives, the row
  shows S − P and the distance it gives, for example "S − P = 6.7 s → 52 km".
- **The circles:** each distance becomes a dashed circle around its station.

| To do this | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Start or pause | **▶ Play** (it turns into **❚❚ Pause**) |
| Start again | **↺ Restart** |
| Change the speed | **Speed**: real time, 2×, 4× (the start) or 8× faster |
| Step through time | Drag the **Time** slider |
| Move the quake | Click the map (tap it on a phone or tablet); it plays again by itself |
| Move the quake with the keyboard | Press **Tab** until the map has a blue outline, then the **arrow keys** (5 km a step; hold **Shift** for 20 km). It plays again when you stop |
| Hide the circles | Untick **Distance circles** |

Two blue labels point these out: **Click the map to move the quake** on the map, and **Drag to
step through time** beside the slider. Each one fades once you have tried it. When you reach
the map with **Tab**, its label lists the keys instead. A screen reader says where the quake
is and which station is nearest after each move.

## Predict, then watch

Write down your prediction before you press Play.

1. Which station will feel the P wave first? Which will wait longest between P and S?
2. Click the map right next to one station. What will its S − P be?
3. Click a corner of the map, outside the stations. Will the circles still cross at the quake?
   At steep or shallow angles? ([Why it matters](../theory/locating.md#why-the-stations-positions-matter))

## A cartoon, not real data

To keep the idea clear, the quake is at the surface (real Utah quakes are mostly 2–15 km
deep), all the rock is the same (real rock is layered), and the seismograms are made up (real
ones are [messier](../theory/seismograms.md)). So the circles cross exactly at the quake; with
real picks they [never quite do](../theory/locating.md#circles-never-meet-perfectly). For
real seismograms, try [Quake Locator](wave-race.md).
